Why this hour?

Of every station-hour in the three days of this storm, none was heavier than this one. Kaiholena (0231), upslope of the Naʻalehu area in Kaʻū, was pounded with 4.37 inches in the sixty minutes 7:30 to 8:30 PM HST, more than half again as much as the next-most-intense downpour recorded anywhere on the island that hour, 2.64 in/hr at Kona Hema. Weather radar indicated 2.67 in/hr falling on Nāʻālehu itself.

The time of the deluge recorded at Kaiholena coincided with the start of the Nāʻālehu flood. While the amount of rain falling in the hours leading up to the peak matters, flash flooding is often a response to a burst of high intensity rainfall, more so than the storm total rainfall. Kaiholena's daily total of 24.41 inches on August 15 is quite high, but ranks only fourth on the island for that day. The island's wettest gauge for the day was Laupāhoehoe with 32.12 inches, nearly a third more rain, but the hourly rate at the time Nāʻālehu was getting its peak rainfall was lower at 1.95 inches per hour.
